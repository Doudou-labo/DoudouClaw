Based on current data, AI is paradoxically creating employment disruption rather than talent shortages in France and the USA, with declining hiring for entry-level tech workers despite broader economic concerns about automation.

## Current Employment Trends

**Employment is declining in tech sectors despite AI demand.** France is experiencing an unprecedented decline in IT employment since 2023—the first drop recorded since 2002[1]. In the fourth quarter of 2025, young workers (ages 15-29) saw a 7.4% annual decline in IT jobs in France, compared to a 0.7% decline across the broader private sector[2]. Similar employment contractions are being observed in the United States[1].

This counterintuitive pattern reflects how companies are adjusting to AI: they are **reducing new hiring and entry-level recruitment** rather than immediately eliminating existing positions[2]. According to the French statistical institute INSEE, "the employment adjustment regarding AI is first materializing through a slowdown in entries and recruitment for early-career positions rather than through generalized contraction."[2]

## Future Automation Threats

Looking ahead, a Coface study estimates that approximately **one in six jobs in France (5 million positions) could become automatable within three to four years**[2]. The sectors most exposed include:

- Architecture and engineering: 26.9% of jobs threatened[5]
- IT and mathematics: 24.9%[5]
- Life sciences and social sciences: 24.7%[5]
- Arts, design, entertainment, and media: 23.8%[5]

Notably, **higher-paid, qualified positions appear most vulnerable**—the top 10% of earners face 22.1% exposure to automation[5].
